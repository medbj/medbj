After a loss, people may offer a lot of ideas about what you should feel. You may also have expectations of your own. Perhaps you think you should be crying more, coping better, or feeling different by now.

There is no single experience that every grieving person must have. This article is a starting point, not a timetable for what should happen next.

## Leave room for different feelings

You might feel sadness, anger, relief, numbness, or several things close together. You may want to talk about the person or situation you have lost, or you may not know what to say.

You do not have to make your feelings fit someone else's account. You can describe your experience in your own words, including “I do not know what I feel.”

## Ask for the kind of company you need

Some days you may want someone to listen. On another day, practical help may matter more. A person might be able to bring food, help with paperwork, or sit with you during a difficult task.

If it helps, be specific: “Could you listen without giving advice?” or “Could you help me make this call?” You can change your mind about what feels useful.

## Let reminders have their place

Dates, places, and ordinary objects may bring the loss to mind. You might want a small ritual, a conversation, or a little privacy. There is no requirement to mark every date or share every memory.

Your culture, beliefs, relationships, and circumstances may shape what feels right. You do not need to explain those choices to every person who asks.

## Find support that respects your experience

Consider a qualified professional or bereavement support service if you want help talking about the loss, if daily life feels hard to manage, or if you are concerned about how you are feeling. You do not have to wait for a particular amount of time before asking.

If you feel unsafe, seek urgent local help. Our [Get Help page](/get-help/) explains routes to immediate support without assuming where you live.
